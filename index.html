not is not !

not $a || $b reads as "not a, or b" - it parses as not($a || $b). The english operators sit at the very bottom of the table, below comparisons, below assignment. That is the whole point of them, and the whole trap.

Your string functions eat expressions

uc $s . $t is not uc of s, then concat. The named unary gobbles the whole concatenation: uc($s . $t) = "FOOBAR". And x is multiplicative while . is additive, so $s x $a . $t repeats first.

Minus loses to power

-$a ** $b is -($a ** $b), never (-$a) ** $b. And <=> is one of the only two truly non-associative operators: chaining it is a compile error, while chaining == silently works left-to-right.
